SW-DGO

Socially-Weighted
Distributed Graph Optimization

Multi-agent route planning for autonomous service fleets — shopping carts, hospital pushchairs, airport trolleys — moving through space full of people who are under no obligation to get out of the way.

The problem

Pedestrians are not obstacles

A shortest-path planner will drive straight through a browsing shopper's personal space, because nothing in its objective says not to. Reactive avoidance only helps once someone is already close — by then the intrusion has happened.

Symmetric conflicts have no local fix

Two carts entering the same single-file aisle from opposite ends is not a collision-avoidance problem. It is a coordination problem, and a purely local controller can only oscillate.

The formulation

Each agent minimises four weighted soft terms over its own directed cost graph, subject to one hard feasibility constraint, solved by D* Lite incremental repair. 

minimise  C(u,v,t) = w_D · D(u,v)         // Euclidean distance
                   + w_M · W_mesh(u,v,t)   // V2V anticipatory congestion
                   + w_H · H_prox(v,t)     // human proxemics
                   + w_S · S_trolley(v,t)  // clearance envelope

subject to  (u,v) ∉ E_reserved(t)          // corridor reservation

Reservation is a constraint, not a fifth weight: a reserved edge is unavailable at any coefficient and an unreserved one contributes zero, so a weight on it would have no operative magnitude in any reachable state.

What the evidence shows

0.00 person-seconds of intimate-space exposure (median)
6.40 for a matched-controller shortest path
4,650 kinodynamic trials, eleven experiments

Social routing does the work

A 2×2 factorial with the mesh and reservation disabled in every cell isolates the proxemic cost term: enabling it alone cuts exposure by 6.40 person-seconds (95% CI [−6.48, −6.31], p = 1.5 × 10⁻⁹), costing 19.03 s of travel time. Reactive yielding adds no detectable reduction on top of it.

The distributed layer is conditional

An ordinary human-aware planner with no mesh and no reservation matches that compliance exactly (p = 1) while finishing ~8 s sooner. The distributed machinery earns its cost only under the topologies it was designed for. We report this rather than omit it.

Honest limits

  • Simulated pedestrians do not react to the robots, so reported exposure is an upper bound on a real crowd.
  • Weights are hand-selected; the sensitivity sweep shows a plateau but claims no optimality.
  • Sensing is range-limited, without ray-casting against shelf occlusion.
  • Fleet scaling is bounded — success falls to 78% at twelve carts in 36 × 24 m.
  • ORCA and local MAPF are our own implementations; no conclusion depends on them.
  • Everything is simulation. No physical fleet was deployed.
  • We report when the distributed layer helps, not how often those conditions arise.
